ICC & funding Questions 1 – 4

The Initial Capital Contribution

What residents think the ICC should be, who should pay it, and how future projects should be funded. Percentages are of the 35 submissions, not of the community.

Question 1

The Board is considering increasing the Initial Capital Contribution (ICC) paid by new purchasers from $50,000 to $75,000. Do you support this proposed increase?

  • Yes, I support increasing the ICC to $75,000 17 · 48.6%
  • I would support an increase, but to an amount below $75,000 1 · 2.9%
  • No, I prefer keeping the ICC at $50,000 12 · 34.3%
  • I need more information before forming an opinion 5 · 14.3%

What it adds up to Six takeaways

Key takeaways

  • Nearly half (48.6%) support raising the ICC for new purchasers to $75,000. About a third (34.3%) prefer keeping it at $50,000, and 14.3% want more information first.
  • Most (82.9%) say an owner who sells and buys another YARCOBR unit to live in should pay no ICC. For an owner adding a second unit as an investment, the most common answer (48.6%) is the full ICC.
  • The ICC is the clear first choice (65.7%) for funding future capital improvements, well ahead of special or regular assessments (11.4% each).
  • 91.4% say a formal 5–10 year capital improvement plan is very or somewhat important.
  • The top priorities are building infrastructure (51.4%), the entrance and guardhouse (42.9%) and beach facilities (40.0%).
  • About two thirds (65.7%) are very or somewhat satisfied with the direction YARCOBR is heading; 20% are dissatisfied.

Moving within YARCOBR

Say no ICC

82.9%

The strongest agreement in the survey: 29 of 35 residents say an owner who sells and buys another YARCOBR unit as their home should not pay the ICC again.

What residents wrote

21 of the 35 respondents answered the open question about the single most important issue for leadership. The most common themes were the Chickee Bar’s food and its management, finishing current projects before starting new ones (and scheduling major work out of season), and long-term structural needs such as concrete restoration and the marina seawall.

Several residents also raised the level of HOA fees, transparency about how past assessments were spent, and having owners involved in major decisions. Others asked for updated landscaping and common areas, better night lighting on walkways, and a year-round Chickee Bar. All 21 comments appear word for word in the full results PDF.

The survey was voluntary and residents chose whether to take part, so these results are not a scientific sample or an official community vote.

Community summary

Residents overwhelmingly value what makes YARCOBR unique — its waterfront lifestyle, private beach, marina, tropical setting, and strong sense of community. Many described the community itself as the primary reason they bought here.

The most frequent suggestions were enhancing the Chickee Bar dining experience, modernizing common areas, expanding guest amenities such as showers and lockers, and continuing to improve communication between residents, management, and the Board.

Overall, the survey reflects a community proud of where it lives and eager to see thoughtful investment that preserves YARCOBR's character while improving the resident experience.
